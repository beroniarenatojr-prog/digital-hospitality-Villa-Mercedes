# Customer workspace, QR deposits, and post-stay feedback

## Operation

Customers use **My reservations**, **New reservation**, **My Inquiries**, **Notifications**, and **Profile**. The account's sections are named exactly once, in `x-customer.account-nav`, which the customer layout renders at the top of every signed-in page. **Browse the resort** in the header returns to the public marketing pages; the header itself carries no section links, so "where am I" is never answered twice with different words.

The one page without that nav is a booking in progress (`customer.booking.*`): the wizard's own four-step indicator is already showing progress, and a second bar competing with it is how a guest loses their place. Each account page announces itself through `x-customer.page-header`, and a single reservation's **Your stay**, **Arrival** and **Payments** are tabs of `x-customer.reservation-tabs` rather than hand-written back links. The first two are facets of one record — `?view=stay` (the default) and `?view=arrival` — so a booking answers one question at a time; the printable arrival pass is a document linked from **Arrival**, and the money keeps its own page. The pass itself is issued only once the booking is confirmed and paid — `Reservation::canShowArrivalPass()` is the gate, and `arrivalPassNotice()` is what both the tab and the saved pass link say while it is shut.

The selection page can be filtered by the guest's own dates, party size and cottage session, and then passes all of them to the existing authenticated booking form. Filtering is done by the availability rules themselves (`AvailabilitySearchService::openAmong`, the same rules the public availability search and the wizard use), so a card that survives the filter is a space the resort will actually accept — and a space under maintenance, or a cottage whose session is taken, is not offered. Each service tab states how many spaces it has free for those dates, so an empty tab points at one that is not. The list that survives is ordered by what the guest actually asked for rather than by name (`FacilityQueryService::rankForGuests`): spaces that take the whole party inside their included count first, since the rate on their card is then the rate the guest pays, then the lowest published starting rate, then a space with a photo over one without, with the name only as the final tie-break so a second page is still a continuation of the first. A space with no published rate ranks after one that has a rate rather than passing as free, and the page states the order it used. Nothing is held by filtering: the wizard revalidates availability and prices the stay on arrival, and a malformed date is refused rather than guessed. A guest who finds nothing is not left with "try again", either: the days either side of their window are checked against the same rules — for the service and the party they asked with — and the nearest dates that do have space are offered as dates to click, keeping the tab, the party and any cottage session (`AvailabilitySearchService::nearbyOpenWindows`). A service with nothing published on it is never told its neighbours are booked, because there was nothing there to book. Public discovery and the existing login/signup continuation remain available.

Every total the wizard prints is explained rather than asserted. The lines carry the arithmetic they are made of when it divides evenly — one definition, `resources/js/price-change.js`, rendered by `customer/booking/partials/price-summary.blade.php` in the confirm-step breakdown, beside the form and in the bar a mobile guest leaves with — and the figure says whether it is an estimate from the published rates or the resort's confirmed total, because the two can differ. Any movement then says what the total was, what it is now, and what moved it: the guest's own edits by name (the party stayed still, the hour count went up), plus the resort's rate when a component moved that the guest never touched, which is the one change that would otherwise happen in silence. A line whose amount does not divide evenly is shown without an arithmetic, the module preferring silence to an explanation that is not true.

A booking left unfinished is resumed rather than restarted. Each step the wizard has had priced leaves a draft in the session of the account that asked for it (`App\Support\BookingDraft`, keyed by account and space), carrying the dates, guest count, extra hours, companion names and preferences; `customer.booking.create` offers it back on the step the guest left, re-prices it on the way in (`calculate` still revalidates availability and the total), and the page says so, with one click to **Start over**. A link that arrives carrying its own dates is a fresh intent and wins over the draft. Confirming a reservation clears the draft, a draft whose arrival has already passed is dropped, and nothing is stored for a visitor who is not signed in — a draft holds no hold on inventory and never carries the guest sheet, whose fields are read from the profile at submission.

A session is one browser, so a guest who reaches the **confirm** step and leaves is also emailed a link back to it (`App\Services\BookingResumeLink`, `App\Jobs\SendBookingResumeLink`). Reaching that step arms the link and schedules the email for `resort.booking.resume_delay_minutes` after the guest's *last* activity on it; the job then asks whether the booking is still unfinished, so the only guest who hears from us is one who actually walked away. Confirming, going back to change the dates, or pressing **Start over** cancels it without a word (`BookingDraft::forget` clears both the session draft and the link). The link (`customer.booking.resume`) puts the draft back into whatever browser it is opened in, is only honoured for the account it was emailed to, works for `resort.booking.resume_expire_hours`, and is not a hold: availability and the total are calculated again on arrival, and a guest who finished the booking in another tab is shown that booking instead of being offered it a second time. The confirm step also carries an **Email me this booking now** button, which sends the same link immediately (and is rate limited), so the feature still works on a deployment whose queue is synchronous — the promise of an automatic email is only made when the queue is asynchronous.

A booking left unfinished is also listed where the guest looks for their bookings: **My reservations** shows it above the tabs, marked **Not submitted** with the space, the dates, the party and any companion names saved, and two actions — continue, or discard. It is deliberately not dressed as a reservation: nothing was submitted, nothing is held, and no deposit is due (`App\Services\Customer\UnfinishedBookings`). It reads both places a draft can live and prefers the fresher one: the browser's own draft where there is one, and the emailed link where the browser is gone, so a guest on another device can still find the work. A draft that can no longer be finished is not offered — a space that has been taken offline is hidden but kept, since a space goes offline and comes back, and a draft for a space that has been deleted is discarded — and a cottage draft is left to the resort page, which is where cottage bookings actually finish.

Each card in that list opens the page that matters for where the booking is (`App\Enums\ReservationDestination`): a pending booking — whose staff review is the open question — opens its deposit and payment history, which is where the 50% deposit will open the moment the request is approved, a confirmed booking that has not finished opens **Arrival**, and a booking that is over opens the record itself. The rule is named once and asked by the card rather than re-derived per list, and no card carries two links to the same page: a booking whose deposit can be paid right there shows the payment button alone.

A submission is a request, not a booking: confirming it lands the guest on **My reservations** under **Review pending**, and nothing is payable yet. New standard online bookings save their server-calculated price and a 50% deposit, rounded up to one centavo, and their review window ends at the earlier of 24 hours after submission or the visit's start — a request nobody answers inside that window releases its space, and the guest is emailed. Approving the request is what opens the 50% QR deposit (`Reservation::canAcceptGuestPayment()` is the only gate, and it requires `Approved`); approval sets a fresh deposit deadline with the same 24-hour shape, again capped at the visit's start, so the guest's clock starts when they are told to pay and not before. An approved booking whose deadline passes with nothing attached is released by `reservations:expire-unpaid`, exactly as an unanswered request is. QR accounts must be activated by an administrator before these holds can be created. Function halls retain staff quotation and approval; historical reservations have no new expiry deadline.

The database continues to store payment instants in the application timezone. New deposit deadlines interpret booking dates as Philippine resort wall-clock times; deadlines and new payment-date controls display Philippine time. Historical reservation dates are not rewritten.

### Staff payment procedure

1. Open **Resort QR accounts**. Upload the actual resort PNG/JPG, account name, account number, and bank where relevant. Check the recipient in the payment app and explicitly attest that the account belongs to the resort before activation. Replace by uploading another account and disabling the old one. Old payment snapshots remain intact. Disabled QR images are not offered for new transfers; eligible existing bookings can still report a transfer made before the account was retired.
2. Open **Payment review**. The oldest waiting proofs come first. A warning identifies proofs waiting more than 24 hours. Inspect the proof (read the transfer reference and date from the uploaded slip when needed), submitted amount, required deposit, receiving-account snapshot, deadline, and duplicate-reference/receipt warnings.
3. **Approve** or decline the request first — that is the decision, and the approval email is what tells the guest the 50% QR deposit is open and until when. Then verify the transfer only after matching the credit and amount in the resort's own receiving account. Verifying the deposit records the money and earns the arrival pass; it does not confirm anything by itself. Verification can occur after the original deadline when the proof was timely; the space remains held during review.
4. Verify a real partial transfer as its actual submitted amount. A remaining deposit shortfall is shown to the customer, with a fresh deadline capped at the visit start, and **Request deposit top-up** sends instructions for it. A shortfall left unpaid past that deadline does not release the booking on its own — verified funds need a person to reconcile them — while an approved booking with no deposit at all is released when its deadline passes, unless the stay has already begun: a confirmation whose guest is on the property is settled at the desk (record the payment and check in), never released by the timer. Never mark an unsupported larger amount as received.
5. Reject with a reason only after confirming the claimed funds were not received. Uncertain or potentially received transfers must stay pending while staff reconcile them. Rejected proofs can be corrected within the updated deadline. A partial verified deposit prevents automatic release even when its top-up deadline passes.
6. Use **Record on-site payment** inside a reservation for money actually collected. Record amount, method, collection time, transfer reference where applicable, and the collecting administrator. The remaining reservation balance must be recorded before **Check in guest** succeeds. Do not add food, damage, or other incidentals to the original reservation price; these remain in the resort's separate incidental-charge register. This change does not introduce a point-of-sale system.
7. For late transfers to expired/cancelled bookings, use the same staff payment record with a reconciliation note. The money stays in history without silently restoring the expired booking or claiming dates now held by someone else. Staff must arrange the actual refund or a new available booking outside this automated confirmation path.
8. Pending proofs prevent cancellation/rejection that would release availability. Received deposits require staff reconciliation before the resort cancels the booking. Paid bookings do not offer customer date changes; staff must reconcile the existing payment before arranging a changed booking.

Customer proof submissions are capped by the required deposit less verified and pending amounts. Customers cannot submit their remaining balance through the proof endpoint, and new gateway checkout is disabled. Existing gateway return/reconciliation paths and payment history remain available.

## Post-stay feedback

**Check out & complete** makes the reservation eligible for an optional rating. A reservation lock and `review_invited_at` marker prevent repeated checkout/dispatch calls from creating another invitation. One database notification is recorded, with one queued mail invitation when outbound delivery is configured. Log/array mailers do not count as live outbound delivery. The mail notification allows one automatic attempt to avoid resending an ambiguously delivered SMTP message; staff must inspect delivery failures before any manual retry. SMTP cannot provide an absolute exactly-once delivery guarantee.

Customers can submit one 1–5 rating per completed, owned reservation. Comments are optional. The visibility choice is required: **staff only** or **staff may publish with first name and last initial**. Customers may edit until moderation. Reviews are unpublished by default and use the existing GuestReview model and public Reviews page. Published historical reviews remain published.

**Guest reviews** is the moderation queue. Staff can publish eligible feedback or mark it reviewed privately. Removing a published review requires a reason. Low ratings alone are not a removal reason. Staff controls do not edit customer ratings or words. Private feedback cannot be published through a direct request.

## Activation checklist

- Apply the additive `2026_09_13_100000_add_customer_deposit_workflow.php` migration after backing up each target database. It has been applied to the main local Villa Mercedes database with unchanged historical reservation, payment, and published-review counts and totals. Other deployments still need their own backup and migration. Do not reseed production data.
- Keep `PAYMENT_DEPOSIT_WORKFLOW=true` for the new workflow. The false mode exists to preserve historical regression fixtures and is not the intended customer payment policy. Gateway checkout remains disabled in the production configuration.
- Obtain and independently verify the real resort QR codes and receiving accounts. No test account should be activated in the real database.
- Run Laravel's scheduler every minute (`php artisan schedule:run`, using the application's normal host scheduler). The scheduled command is `reservations:expire-unpaid`. For development, `php artisan schedule:work` is an alternative. Verify that the host runs it while the resort is accepting bookings.
- Run the configured queue worker (`php artisan queue:work`) and test the configured outbound mail transport with an authorized recipient. A queued invitation is not proof of email delivery.
- Train staff on pending proofs, overdue review warnings, reconciliation, arrival collection, and review moderation before releasing online reservations.

## Verification

The dedicated `CustomerDepositWorkflowTest` and `CustomerReviewWorkflowTest` explicitly enable the new policy. Existing tests use their historical workflow configuration through `phpunit.xml`; `OnlinePaymentCheckoutTest` explicitly enables its historical gateway fixture. `ReservationConcurrencyTest` includes real competing processes for new holds and deposit submissions.

The full local Laravel suite passed with 699 tests and 2,728 assertions. After the final admin wording change, the focused deposit, review, and decision-board checks passed with 29 tests and 137 assertions. After removing payment-number fallbacks, another 52 customer payment and historical checkout tests passed with 145 assertions. The Vite build, Blade compilation, and three JavaScript UI tests passed. Desktop/mobile customer browser checks and the administrator proof-verification, on-site collection, check-in, checkout, private moderation, and QR-preview walkthrough used an isolated database with conspicuously labelled non-payment fixtures. They did not transfer real funds or send live mail. Verify the host scheduler, queue, mail transport, genuine receiving accounts, and staff procedure before enabling reservations outside this local environment.
